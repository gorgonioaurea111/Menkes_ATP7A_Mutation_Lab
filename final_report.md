# Final Report — From Gene Mutation to Disease

## 1. Title

### From Gene Mutation to Disease: Analysis of ATP7A and Menkes Disease

## 2. Disease Background

Menkes disease is a rare inherited disorder of copper metabolism caused by pathogenic variants in the **ATP7A** gene. It can involve developmental and neurological abnormalities, seizures, failure to thrive, hypotonia, and sparse, brittle, or kinky hair.

The disease is generally inherited in an **X-linked recessive** pattern. The nervous system is particularly affected because proper copper distribution is important for copper-dependent cellular processes.

## 3. Gene and Normal Protein Function

The gene investigated in this laboratory activity is **ATP7A**, located on the X chromosome at **Xq21.1**.

ATP7A encodes a copper-transporting P-type ATPase involved in copper transport and distribution. It helps supply copper to copper-dependent enzymes and contributes to cellular copper homeostasis.

## 4. Documented Mutation

The documented mutation investigated was:

**NM_000052.7(ATP7A):c.2179G>A**

This changes the codon:

**GGA → AGA**

resulting in:

**p.Gly727Arg (G727R)**

## 5. Hypothesis

Because c.2179G>A is a single-nucleotide substitution, the reading frame and overall protein length were expected to remain unchanged.

The mutation was predicted to change glycine at position 727 to arginine, producing the missense substitution **G727R**. This amino-acid substitution may affect ATP7A structure or function.

## 6. Methods

The normal ATP7A CDS was obtained from the reference transcript **NM_000052.7**. The CDS was translated to establish the wild-type protein sequence.

A copy of the wild-type sequence was used to reproduce the documented c.2179G>A mutation. The mutant sequence was translated using the same reading frame and compared with the wild-type protein.

A second artificial mutation was also created by changing the codon **AGT to AGA**, producing the predicted **S4R** amino-acid substitution.

The resulting sequences were compared based on protein length, amino-acid differences, reading frame, and premature stop codons.

## 9. Artificial Mutation Experiment

The artificial mutation was:

**AGT → AGA**

This changes:

**Serine (S) → Arginine (R)**

or:

**S4R**

Like G727R, S4R is a missense single-nucleotide substitution. It does not change the reading frame or protein length and does not produce a premature stop codon.

However, S4R was created computationally in this laboratory activity. Its functional effect was therefore not experimentally demonstrated.

## 10. Molecular Interpretation

The documented mutation demonstrates the following sequence of events:

**c.2179G>A**

↓

**GGA → AGA codon change**

↓

**Gly727Arg amino-acid substitution**

↓

**Potential alteration of ATP7A structure or stability**

↓

**Reduced ATP7A function/copper transport**

↓

**Disturbed cellular copper homeostasis**

↓

**Contribution to the abnormalities associated with Menkes disease**

The computational analysis directly supports the nucleotide and amino-acid sequence changes. Published experimental evidence is required to support conclusions about protein stability, degradation, copper transport, cellular effects, and disease mechanism.

Experimental studies of G727R have reported reduced amounts of mutant ATP7A protein associated with increased degradation and impaired copper-transport activity.

## 11. Limitations

This laboratory activity primarily used sequence analysis and translation to investigate the effects of mutations.

The computational analysis can directly demonstrate nucleotide changes, codon changes, amino-acid substitutions, reading-frame effects, protein length, and premature stop codons.

However, computational sequence analysis alone cannot demonstrate protein folding, protein stability, degradation rate, cellular localization, copper-transport activity, or the complete disease mechanism.

The artificial S4R mutation was not experimentally tested, so its functional consequence should be considered a prediction rather than a demonstrated biological effect.

## 12. Conclusion

The ATP7A c.2179G>A mutation is a missense single-nucleotide substitution that changes GGA to AGA and produces the p.Gly727Arg (G727R) amino-acid substitution.

The mutation does not cause a frameshift, premature stop codon, or change in overall protein length. Instead, it changes one amino acid at position 727.

The artificial S4R mutation produced a similar sequence-level outcome but occurred at a different protein position and was only computationally generated.

Overall, the laboratory demonstrates how a single DNA nucleotide change can produce a specific amino-acid substitution and how sequence-level changes can be connected to possible molecular and cellular consequences. Experimental evidence is necessary to establish the functional and disease-related effects beyond the computational results.
