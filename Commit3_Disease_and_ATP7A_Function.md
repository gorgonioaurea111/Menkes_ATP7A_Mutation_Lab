Menkes Disease and Normal ATP7A Function

Disease: Menkes Disease

Menkes disease is a genetic disorder associated with abnormal copper metabolism. The disease is caused by pathogenic variants in the ATP7A gene.

The disorder can result from reduced or impaired ATP7A function, affecting the normal transport and distribution of copper in cells.

Gene: ATP7A

Gene symbol: ATP7A
Reference transcript: NM_000052.7
Reference protein: NP_000043.4

ATP7A encodes a copper-transporting P-type ATPase. Its normal function is involved in transporting copper across cellular membranes and maintaining copper homeostasis.

Normal Protein Function

The ATP7A protein is involved in copper transport and cellular copper homeostasis. Proper ATP7A function is important for supplying copper to copper-dependent processes while maintaining appropriate intracellular copper levels.

Mutation Investigated

The documented mutation investigated in this project is:

ATP7A c.2179G>A → p.Gly727Arg (G727R)

This is a single-nucleotide substitution that produces a missense change in the ATP7A protein.

Project Focus

This project examines how a nucleotide change in ATP7A can produce an amino-acid substitution and how the sequence-level change may relate to changes in protein function and the Menkes disease phenotype.
